Chapter one

Where we began

More than a year now, Tahira. Somewhere between the arguments we didn't mean and the laughter we couldn't stop, you quietly became the person I plan my whole life around.

The beginning — two people who somehow already knew each other's silences, and never needed to explain the small things.

The small fights — we've debated, gone quiet, sulked a little, and come back anyway — every single time — because leaving was never actually on the table.

The everyday — ordinary calls, forwarded reels, and "did you eat yet" messages that somehow became the best part of my day.

The hard days — the ones where you were upset with me and I felt it in my chest for hours — and I learned, slowly, how to be gentler.

The good days — which, honestly, were most of them — you, me, and a whole world that felt lighter because we were in it together.

Right now — one year and more in, still choosing you, still certain, still yours.

Will you marry me?

Every fight, every reel, every quiet Tuesday — led here. Meri jaan, my whole world, my forever.

 

To my Tahira,

You are the whole world I somehow get to hold in one pair of hands. Every little thing — a message, a laugh, a five-minute call between chores — still feels like the best part of my day, even after a year of it.

When you're upset with me, something in my chest goes quiet, and I carry it longer than I let on. When I'm the one who's low, I go too far inward, and I know that's not fair to you either. So here's my promise, plainly: I will keep choosing you, keep showing up softer after every disagreement, and never let a fight matter more than us.

I want to complete every wish we've ever spoken about — the small ones like a lazy Sunday with no phones, and the big one, InshaAllah, of a home that's fully ours.

Tahira, tum hamesha meri ho, aur Israr hamesha tumhara.
I love you, and I don't want a single second without you.

Take care of yourself the way you take care of everyone else. And — take care of me too, jaan. That's all I'm asking for, along with forever.

Always yours,
Israr
